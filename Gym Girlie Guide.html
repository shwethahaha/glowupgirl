Your week

Seven days, one rhythm

Mon to Fri are strength days. Wednesday is lighter because run club comes first. The weekend session flexes around your second run.

Before you lift

Warm-up, sorted

Your 15-minute walk to the gym counts as your warm-up. No extra treadmill time needed.

The walk in

  • 15 minutes at a comfortable pace
  • Build to a brisk, purposeful walk by the end
  • Skip the 5 to 8 minute treadmill warm-up

Before your first lift

  • Do 1 to 3 lighter warm-up sets of that exercise
  • Add weight a little each set until you reach working weight
  • Warm-up sets do not count toward the sets in this guide

Quick extras

  • Lower-body day, legs stiff: 1 to 2 minutes of hip circles, leg swings, bodyweight squats
  • Upper-body day: a few arm circles and gentle shoulder rolls
  • Wednesday: the run warms you up, but still do a light practice set
Walking home is your cool-downOn days when you have already done enough, skip the extra treadmill cardio and let the walk home do the job.

Gym girlie rules

Every session

Effort

Pick weights that leave 2 to 3 good reps in the tank. You never need to hit failure.

Rest between sets

90 to 120 seconds after big compound lifts. 60 to 90 seconds after smaller exercises.

Form first

Controlled reps and a comfortable range of motion. Add weight only when your technique stays consistent.

Weekend athletic day

Alternate Saturday and Sunday around your run club. No heavy legs or upper body today. Just move and enjoy it. You never need to make up a missed session.

Gym day ritual

Steam room reset

Your weekend gym session ends in the steam room. It is a relaxing finish, not a workout.

  1. Drink firstHave water before you go in, and again afterwards.
  2. Cool downLet your heart rate settle after your workout before you step in.
  3. Keep it shortAim for 10 to 15 minutes. Step out earlier if you want to.
  4. Slow breathsSit low, breathe slowly, and let your shoulders and hips soften.
  5. Cool offStand up slowly, rinse off and let your body temperature come back down.

Listen to your bodyLeave straight away if you feel dizzy, faint or unwell. Skip the steam on days you are ill or very dehydrated. If you have a health condition, ask your doctor if steam rooms are right for you.

Your first 8 weeks

How to get stronger

Weeks 1 to 2

Learn

Use manageable weights, practise technique and get comfy with the machines. If it feels like a lot, start with 2 working sets per exercise.

Weeks 3 to 5

Progress

Add reps while your form stays clean. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, go up by the smallest weight step.

Weeks 6 to 8

Evaluate

Compare weights, reps, running stamina, energy and recovery with week 1. You do not need to progress every exercise every session.

Wellness and recovery

Rest is part of the plan

With six gym days and two runs, recovery matters as much as the lifting.

Sleep

7 to 9 hours

Most of your strength and fitness gains happen while you sleep. Protect bedtime on run-club mornings.

Water

Sip all day

Carry a bottle to the gym, drink during sessions, and top up after the steam room.

Protein

Every meal

Include a protein source at each meal, plus a snack with carbs and protein after harder sessions.

Stretch

5 minutes

After lifting, stretch whatever you worked: quads, hamstrings, glutes, chest and upper back.

Walk

Easy days

Your walk to and from the gym keeps you moving and helps muscles feel less stiff.

Steam

Weekend treat

Your weekend steam room visit helps you unwind. Keep it short and hydrate well.

Dial it down if

  • Soreness from Monday or Wednesday is still there on Thursday: drop a set or swap the step-ups for a seated move
  • Run club was longer or harder than planned: do 3 exercises on Wednesday or skip the gym
  • You feel flat or unusually tired: take a full rest day

Stop and reassess if you have persistent joint pain, dizziness or unusual exhaustion.

More recovery

Recovery menu

Pick one or two a day. None of these are homework.

Foam roll

5 to 10 minutes on quads, glutes and upper back after Monday and Thursday lower-body days.

Legs up the wall

Lie on your back with legs resting up a wall for 5 minutes. Easy way to unwind after a run or leg day.

Warm bath or shower

A long warm soak on Thursday night helps tired legs feel looser. Add Epsom salts if you like them.

Mobility flow

10 minutes of hip openers, cat-cow, thoracic rotations and a gentle hamstring stretch on rest days or before bed.

Breathing reset

Try 5 slow breaths: in for 4 counts, out for 6. Great after the steam room or before sleep.

Massage or massage gun

A weekly massage or a few minutes with a massage gun on calves, quads and glutes. Skip sore joints and bony areas.

Morning sunlight

10 minutes outside after waking helps you feel alert and supports better sleep at night.

Easy active recovery

A relaxed walk, gentle swim or slow stretch session keeps you moving without adding stress.

Meal prep

Prep a few protein-rich meals and snacks on a quieter day so post-workout fuel is ready.

Glow-up activities

The glow-up menu

Feeling good counts as part of the plan. Tick off the ones you do this week.

Skin

  • Cleanse after sweaty sessions
  • Moisturiser and SPF on walk-to-gym days
  • Sheet mask or clay mask after the steam room
  • Lip balm and hydrating mist in your bag

Hair

  • Use a silk scrunchie or soft clip for workouts
  • Weekly hair mask or oil treatment
  • Scalp massage in the shower
  • Try a sleek gym braid or bun you love

Body

  • Body scrub before a shower
  • Moisturise after the steam room
  • Soak your feet after a long run
  • Treat yourself to a manicure or pedicure

Mind

  • Journal 3 wins from your week
  • Phone-free wind-down before bed
  • Make a feel-good gym playlist
  • Plan one fun outing: a new class, cafe or climbing gym

Style

  • Pick a gym outfit that makes you feel great
  • Refresh your workout set or trainers when you need to
  • Keep a cute water bottle and towel

Progress

Take a quick note of your weights and how you felt each week. Photos or measurements are optional, and only if they feel fun and motivating.
